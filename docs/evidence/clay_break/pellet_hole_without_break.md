# Pellet hole / pellet contact without scoring-visible break

## Recovery status: FOUND

**Meaning of FOUND:** the underlying phenomenon has been directly reported in a structured recovered-target experiment and is independently supported by recovered unbroken-target inspection data.

**FOUND does not mean:** universal frequency known; mechanism calibrated; every pellet hole is non-scoring; or a BangLab break model is validated.

## Recovery target

Recover evidence for the remembered phenomenon that a shotgun pellet can strike or perforate a clay target without causing a fragment visible to the referee/shooter and therefore without necessarily producing a scoring hit.

## Primary recovery — Roster 2018

Tom Roster's *Clay Target Nation* article describes a deliberate recovery protocol.

Standard White Flyer targets were shot as left-to-right crossers. The ammunition was Winchester AA 20-gauge 7/8 oz No. 9 lead shot through a skeet-choked Remington 3200. Targets were 25–30 yd from the gun when struck.

Apparent misses were physically recovered and inspected. Targets showing no pellet holes or tiny pellet-caused chips were rejected from the struck sample and replacement targets were fired until **100 demonstrably struck targets** had been accumulated. Targets showing one or more pellet holes or tiny pellet-caused chips were classified as struck.

Of those 100 No. 9 struck targets, **5% produced no chip visible from the shooting station** and, according to the author, would have been recorded lost in the skeet shoot.

The test was repeated with No. 8 shot in the same general Winchester AA load. In that 100-target struck sample, the number without a visible chip was reported as **zero**.

Source: Tom Roster, “The Best Loads for Skeet and Sporting Clays,” *Clay Target Nation*, April 2018.  
Recovery URL: https://nssa-nsca.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/CTN0418_FE_TestingLoads.pdf

### What this directly establishes

- Some targets can be demonstrably struck yet fail to shed a chip visible from the shooting station.
- A pellet hole is evidence of physical interaction/penetration but is not by itself proof of a scoring-visible fragment.
- Under this test, the phenomenon occurred in the No. 9 struck-target sample.

### What it does not establish

- It does not provide the number of pellet holes per each of the five non-visible-chip targets.
- It does not give individual pellet impact velocities or energies.
- It does not isolate pellet diameter from hardness, retained velocity, deformation, pattern characteristics or exact hit location.
- It does not establish a universal 5% failure rate for No. 9 shot.
- It does not establish the current 2026 CPSA scoring treatment of a visible hole.

## Independent recovery — conventional pitch targets

US6428007B2 reports pickup inspection of **unbroken** conventional pitch targets after shooting at 27 yd.

The exact observed pellet-mark distribution among recovered unbroken conventional pitch targets was:

Source: Brian Skeuse & Rupert Spencer, *Environmentally safe projectable targets*, US6428007B2.  
Recovery URL: https://patents.google.com/patent/US6428007B2/en

This independently establishes the broader physical phenomenon: an unbroken conventional pitch target may bear one or multiple observed pellet marks. One recovered unbroken target bore seven marks.

The patent calls these **pellet marks**; this record does not silently upgrade every mark to a through-hole or penetration.

## Scoring distinction

The physical phenomenon and the scoring endpoint must remain separate.

For the inspected ISSF rules, a visible piece broken from the target is the normal HIT criterion; dust without a visible piece is not a HIT. FITASC inspected rules similarly use visible-piece criteria.

A CPSA 2026 Booklet 1 edition has been identified, but its current scoring wording was **not inspected in this recovery record**. The inspected CPSA text is the 2023 edition. Historical CPSA provisions concerning holes must not be assumed to remain current in 2026.

Therefore the durable statement is:

> **Physical contact or penetration can occur without a scoring-visible fragment. The scoring consequence must be evaluated under the applicable rule version and what the referee actually observes.**

## Epistemic classification

- Roster 100-target No. 9 result: **MEASURED practitioner experiment**.
- Roster No. 8 repeat: **MEASURED practitioner experiment**.
- Skeuse/Spencer pellet-mark distribution: **MEASURED as disclosed in a primary patent experiment**.
- General statement that CONTACT ≠ SCORING DAMAGE: **supported by direct negative observations**.
- Universal probability or energy threshold: **NOT RECOVERED**.

## BangLab consequence

This record authorises no model change. It establishes a falsifier that any future break/scoring model must survive: a model that deterministically maps every pellet contact, every penetration, or every multi-pellet contact to scoring damage is inconsistent with recovered observations.
